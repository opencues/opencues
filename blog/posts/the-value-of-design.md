# The Value of Design

In the age of AI it is interesting to circle back to an old question I used to ponder.

> How do you determine the value of a design?

After pondering for a while I came up with an equation:

Perceived Quality x Perceived Uniqueness = Perceived Value

The equation contains values ranging from 0-1.

Prior to AI generated designs of all kinds, I believe this equation was a good axiom to work with as it captured the phenomenon where you liked a particular design until you started to over-index on aspects of the design. This process would be lower your perceived uniqueness of the design.

Prior to AI, quality was heavily constrained by the supply of designers around the world now the overall quality of design around the world has increased by some objective number however there is still an overall low 'perceived value' for designs around the world.

I believe this could be due to the 'Perceived Uniqueness' metric being lowered by the abundance of similar designs which drags down the 'Perceived Value'.

It is an interesting equation to reference when you find yourself observing a design of sound quality yet not feeling enamoured by it due to the perceived 'sloppiness' of it.

In a world where replication is near instant the need for continuous innovation and adaption is necessary since Perceived uniqueness value decays faster than ever.
