# Background

This invention describes ways that an AI system can provide feedback during turn-based communication.

## Background

Current AI systems are turn-based: the user types a prompt that the system responds to. Human interaction is not strictly turn-based: we use a wide array of methods for communicating information to another party even when it is not our turn to speak, such as facial expressions. These feedback methods increase communication efficiency, because the listener can steer or shape the ongoing speech of the speaker without requiring the speaker to end their turn.

The core innovation enabling this is that the non-speaking party is communicating through a different modality than the speaking party. This prevents the non-speaking party's communication from drowning out the speaking-party's communication.

Examples of the negative implications of using the same modality of communication to communicate at the same time:

- Two individuals speak over each other and do not take turns, often resulting in both parties becoming more frustrated and misunderstood.
- Two individuals texting each other at the same time resulting in hard to follow conversation flow due to the order of messages being mixed up, leading to confusion.

To overcome these potential pitfalls humans often implement non-verbal cues.

Human communication is only faux-turn based due to the existence of non-verbal cues which allow an individual to get their point across without interrupting another individual's turn to communicate.

An individual communicating to someone who is telegraphing through non-verbal cues can choose to omit information or change the information they are communicating based on the information telegraphed by a non-speaking party through non-verbal cues. Analysing and interpreting non verbal cues can prevent an individual from miscommunicating or can help steer an individual towards communication which is pleasurable to the non-speaking party. Based on the non-verbal cues telegraphed by the non-speaking party a speaking party may decide to prematurely end their turn, resulting in both parties saving time Based on the non-verbal cues telegraphed by the non-speaking party a speaking party may be informed of a piece of information without being interrupted.

Albert Mehrabian, suggests that 55% of communication is body language, 38% is tone of voice, and only 7% is the actual words spoken.
